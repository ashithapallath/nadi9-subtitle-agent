# Nadi-9 Grammar Note (v1, compiled 2024-03-01, author: Field Linguistics Team)

1. **Word order**: Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) is dominant, but topicalized elements may move to sentence end
   for emphasis or emotional weight (e.g. address terms like "dada" often appear sentence-final or
   sentence-initial depending on emotional register — INCOMPLETE, only 6 supporting examples).
2. **Negation**: formed with particle "na" placed before the verb. High confidence (12+ examples).
3. **Tense**: past tense often marked with "-li"/"-ili" suffix on the verb stem. Future uses "-bi"/"-be".
   Present/habitual uses "-chi"/"-uchi". Moderate confidence.
4. **Politeness / respect register**: addressing a senior or formal listener ("sahib") triggers a distinct
   verb form (e.g. "ausibe" instead of "aasibu"). Only 2 examples support this — LOW CONFIDENCE, needs more data.
5. **Kinship + relationship-state interaction**: preliminary field notes suggest that the same kinship
   address term ("dada") may co-occur with different verb-final particles depending on the emotional
   state of the relationship (conflict vs. reconciliation), but the grammar note does NOT resolve this
   fully. Flagged by the field team as "requires expert review."
6. **Code-switching**: speakers occasionally insert source-language loanwords for modern objects
   (phones, documents) with no attested Nadi-9 replacement. No rule provided for this yet.
